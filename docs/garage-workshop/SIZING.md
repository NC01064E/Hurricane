# Mini-split sizing (hand estimate)

> A first-pass estimate to choose between 12K and 18K. It is **not a Manual J**. Assumptions: Fort Lauderdale summer design (~91 F outdoor, 75 F indoor, attic ~130 F), the insulated ceiling and partition from the plan, and the exterior block walls and door as they are today. ## Result

- **12,000 BTU/h** covers the worst-case peak (~10,520 BTU/h, about 88% of rated capacity) and has margin for the extra heat the estimate ignores.
- At idle (~5,990 BTU/h) the room needs well under half of a 12K unit. A 12K inverter modulates down to roughly 3,000-4,000 BTU/h, (typical for inverter units; check the spec sheet), so it can run long and steady at idle, which is what dehumidifying steel storage needs.
- An 18K unit's minimum output is roughly 5,000-6,000 BTU/h (typical for inverter mini-splits; check the spec sheet), about the whole idle load. It would cycle on and off at idle, which is the short-cycling the plan itself warns against.
- **Do not go to 9K:** the peak load, not idle, sets the size, and 9K would run flat out through a workout.

## What would change the answer

- The uninsulated block walls are the biggest single envelope item (about a fifth of the peak, nearly 40% of idle). Insulating or shading them would cut the peak by roughly 1,000-1,500 BTU/h; 12K is the right call either way.
- A west-facing exterior wall, a larger exterior door, or a third person working out regularly moves the peak toward the top of 12K's range.
- A heat pump's rated capacity falls as outdoor temperature rises; 12,000 BTU/h is the rating at 95 F. If an installer's Manual J exceeds about 11,500 BTU/h, revisit.
